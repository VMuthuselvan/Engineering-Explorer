How Your Match Is Calculated

The Engineering Major Match Meter was developed using established career-development theories and research on engineering major choice. It considers multiple factors, including the activities and problems you enjoy, your vocational interests, work values, preferred ways of learning and working, desired career impact, and areas where you currently feel confident.

The matching approach is informed by:

  • Holland's RIASEC person-environment fit theory — provides a broad structure for understanding vocational interests and the fit between a person's preferences and educational or work environments.
  • Social Cognitive Career Theory — connects learning experiences, self-efficacy, outcome expectations, interests, goals, and contextual supports or barriers.
  • Engineering identity and belonging research — examines interest, competence beliefs, recognition, and whether students see engineering as a place where they belong.
  • Work-values research — helps distinguish students who enjoy similar activities but seek different forms of impact, autonomy, collaboration, or working conditions.

Your responses are compared with documented educational and career characteristics associated with Michigan Engineering disciplines. The Match Meter then identifies several majors that may align with your current profile.

The Match Meter is an exploration tool, not an admissions test, measure of ability, or final career recommendation. Interests, confidence, values, and goals can change as you gain new experiences. Consider these results alongside coursework, advising, and conversations with faculty and students.
